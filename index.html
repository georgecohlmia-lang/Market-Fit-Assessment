STEP 01

Split your customers into two or three groups.

The easiest split is usually how they came to you: a referral, word of mouth, or they came on their own. From here on, every number gets run for each group separately. Never for everybody at once.

Choose the kind of business above. The questions are the same for every kind. How you get the answers is different.
From the lesson: Upstream split four hundred members three ways and checked both things in an afternoon. The lesson gives the results in words, not exact figures or group sizes, so the example keeps its words. The worksheet reads numbers out of phrases like “nine in ten” or “about half,” and leaves the rest without a bar. The lesson doesn’t say what Upstream stopped spending on, so step three is left for you.
STEP 02

For each group, check two things.

Do they stay? What share are still with you after twelve months, or since you opened if that’s sooner. That’s the one that can’t be faked. Do they send people? What share of them have sent you someone. Money can buy attention. It can’t buy that. Rough numbers are fine: a count, a percentage, or “about half.”

Kind words aren’t a measurement

Both checks are things customers do, not things they say. People say generous things right after you’ve helped them, and some of them leave four months later. Gratitude and staying are different measurements. So is a wall of testimonials.

If you don’t know who’s referred whom, start asking every new customer this one question, this week.

STEP 03

Pick the group where both numbers are strongest.

Money, time or attention going to a group that doesn’t stay or send anyone.

Stay with the strong group until you understand why it works. That tells you where to get more of them.

The trap · fixing the weak group first

It feels responsible. But you don’t know yet whether the weak group is fixable or just different. Fixing them is a guess. Understanding the strong group isn’t. It isn’t giving up on part of the business. It’s putting your money where the evidence is.
